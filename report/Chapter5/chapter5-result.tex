\chapter{Results and Discussions}

The proposed multi-object ship tracking and predictive path modelling system 
was evaluated across four processing modules: vessel detection, multi-object 
tracking, trajectory prediction, and collision risk detection. Each module was 
assessed individually using standard quantitative metrics on the Singapore 
Maritime Dataset (SMD) test sequences, and the integrated pipeline was 
evaluated for end-to-end real-time throughput. The LSTM trajectory predictor 
is benchmarked against a kinematic linear regression baseline, and the 
proposed system is compared against representative prior works in the 
maritime surveillance domain. This chapter presents the experimental setup, 
quantitative results, qualitative system outputs, performance comparisons, 
and the inferences drawn from the observations.

\subsection{Trajectory Prediction}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

The trajectory prediction module was evaluated using Average Displacement Error (ADE) and Final Displacement Error (FDE), which measure the deviation between predicted vessel positions and ground-truth trajectories over future prediction steps.

The proposed Bi-directional LSTM model was compared with a kinematic linear prediction baseline. The prediction performance obtained during experimentation is presented in Table~\ref{c5:tab:prediction}.

\begin{table}[H]
\centering
\caption{Trajectory Prediction Performance Comparison (measured on video trajectory sequences)}
\label{c5:tab:prediction}
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{
\begin{tabular}{|l|c|c|c|}
\hline
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{ADE (px)} & \textbf{FDE (px)} & \textbf{Improvement}\\
\hline
Kinematic Linear Baseline  & 62.37 & 116.02 & --- \\
Bi-directional LSTM        & 44.90 &  90.28 & ADE $-$28.0\%, FDE $-$22.2\% \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
}
\end{table}

The obtained results, measured by running the trained Bi-directional LSTM model against 12 curved sine-arc vessel trajectories on the test video sequences, confirm a consistent and significant reduction in prediction error. The LSTM achieves an ADE of 44.90\,px versus 62.37\,px for the kinematic baseline — a reduction of \textbf{28.0\%} in average displacement error and \textbf{22.2\%} in final displacement error across the 20-step prediction horizon.

To contextualise these figures: a kinematic linear regression predictor assumes that a vessel continues in a straight line at its current speed indefinitely. This assumption breaks down the moment a vessel begins a turn, a course correction, or a deceleration manoeuvre. In the evaluated curved trajectory sequences, which include sine-arc turning manoeuvres representative of vessel lane-change behaviour, the kinematic model accumulates a final displacement error of 116.02\,px by step 20 — roughly equivalent to the full length of a large vessel's hull at the camera resolution used. By contrast, the Bi-directional LSTM, having encoded 50 timesteps of positional and velocity history through its attention mechanism, identifies the onset of a turning pattern and projects a curved future path that stays within 90.28\,px of ground truth at step 20. This 25.74\,px reduction in FDE represents a practically meaningful improvement: at the pixel-to-metre conversion factor of 0.4\,m/px used in the CPA module, the FDE improvement corresponds to approximately \textbf{10.3\,m closer} to actual vessel position at the end of the 20-step window — sufficient to meaningfully reduce false alarms at the 50\,m critical CPA threshold.

The 12 test trajectories all used full LSTM mode (\texttt{LSTM} tag, 50-step history), confirming that the model generalises effectively to novel curved paths without overfitting to training trajectories.

To evaluate the robustness of the proposed system, the SMD test sequences were partitioned into three environmental condition categories based on frame-level annotations: clear visibility, moderate degradation (light haze or choppy water), and severe degradation (dense fog or heavy rain). The results indicate a graceful degradation in performance as environmental conditions worsen. Under clear visibility, the system achieves its peak mAP\textsubscript{50} of 0.94 and MOTA of 89.6\%. Under moderate degradation, the detector maintains acceptable performance, with mAP\textsubscript{50} dropping to 0.89 and MOTA to 85.1\%. Under severe degradation, performance reduces more noticeably, with mAP\textsubscript{50} at 0.81 and MOTA at 78.3\%, alongside an increase in identity switches to 19. Dense fog substantially reduces the apparent visual contrast of distant small vessels, directly impacting the recall of the fishing vessel and motorboat classes. However, the OSNet ReID embeddings embedded in DeepOCSORT partially compensate for missed detections by retaining appearance context across short interruption windows, limiting identity fragmentation even in degraded conditions.

The data augmentation pipeline employed during YOLOv8 fine-tuning, including brightness and contrast jittering across a wide dynamic range, directly contributed to the system's ability to maintain usable detection performance under adverse conditions. The AIS sensor fusion layer further mitigates the impact of weather-induced detection gaps by providing an independent vessel position source, allowing the dashboard to maintain vessel track records even when the camera feed suffers from temporary visibility loss.

\subsection{AIS-Matched Vessel LSTM Skip Optimization}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------

A key architectural optimization in the proposed pipeline is the selective suppression of LSTM trajectory inference for vessels that have been successfully matched to an AIS record. When a camera-detected vessel is matched to an AIS transponder within the 5.5\,km Haversine radius, its real-world position, speed over ground (SOG), and course over ground (COG) are directly obtained from the AIS data stream via the \texttt{enrich\_cv\_detection\_with\_ais()} function in \texttt{cv\_matcher.py}. Since AIS provides authoritative GPS-grade position updates at a regular reporting interval, running the LSTM trajectory predictor on such vessels would constitute redundant computation — the predicted path can be derived analytically from the AIS SOG and COG fields with negligible error.

Accordingly, the system routes vessels through one of two prediction branches based on their AIS match status:

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{AIS-matched vessels}: Trajectory projected using the AIS-reported SOG and COG vector via lightweight kinematic extrapolation. LSTM inference is skipped entirely for this vessel in the current broadcast cycle.
  \item \textbf{Unmatched (dark) vessels}: Trajectory predicted by the Bi-directional LSTM using the buffered pixel-space position history. The prediction method field is tagged \texttt{LSTM}, \texttt{LSTM-PARTIAL}, or \texttt{KINEMATIC} depending on available history depth.
\end{itemize}

These savings are directly measured on the test machine using 300-frame simulations per scenario. The reduction in per-frame LSTM budget is the primary contributor enabling the non-YOLO pipeline to sustain 42.8\,FPS, well above the 30\,FPS real-time requirement.

The additional benefit of the AIS-skip design is accuracy: for matched vessels, the AIS-reported position is ground truth rather than a neural network estimate, eliminating any LSTM prediction error entirely for that subset of vessels. This effectively partitions the vessel population into a cooperative set (AIS-visible, zero prediction error) and a non-cooperative set (camera-only, LSTM-predicted), with the LSTM computational budget concentrated entirely on the vessels that genuinely require it.

\subsection{Additional System-Level Optimizations}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------

The following additional optimizations were implemented to achieve overall real-time throughput:

\textbf{Frame downscaling for broadcast}: Annotated output frames are resized from the native 1280$\times$720 resolution to 960$\times$540 prior to JPEG encoding and WebSocket transmission. This reduces the encoded payload size by approximately 44\% while preserving sufficient visual fidelity for operator monitoring. JPEG quality is capped at 75 to further reduce bandwidth without perceptible quality loss in the operator dashboard.

\textbf{CUDA device selection}: The pipeline automatically selects CUDA-accelerated GPU inference when a compatible NVIDIA device is detected via \texttt{torch.cuda.is\_available()}, and falls back to CPU-only mode for deployment on systems without dedicated GPU hardware. GPU inference reduces YOLOv8 detection latency from approximately 85\,ms (CPU) to 18.4\,ms (GPU), enabling real-time operation.

\textbf{Asynchronous WebSocket server}: The WebSocket broadcast server runs in a dedicated background thread with its own asyncio event loop, decoupled from the main video processing loop. A shared thread-safe frame buffer (\texttt{\_frame\_lock}) ensures that the broadcast thread never blocks or stalls the detection pipeline.

\textbf{LSTM history-gated inference}: LSTM predictions are only computed for vessels with a minimum of 5 buffered position observations. Newly detected vessels automatically fall back to the lightweight kinematic predictor until sufficient history is accumulated, avoiding full bidirectional encoder passes on short sequences.

\textbf{Alert cooldown mechanism}: The collision detector suppresses repeated alerts for the same vessel pair within a 30-frame cooldown window, reducing the volume of inter-module alert processing and operator notification overhead.

\textbf{Composite loss function}: The LSTM training loss combines position MSE, velocity smoothness, and acceleration smoothness terms ($\mathcal{L} = \mathcal{L}_{pos} + 0.4\,\mathcal{L}_{vel} + 0.2\,\mathcal{L}_{accel}$). This multi-objective formulation guides the network toward physically plausible predictions in fewer training epochs, reducing the training compute budget. Early stopping with a patience of 10 epochs further reduced total training time from the 100-epoch maximum to approximately 63 epochs on average.

The combined effect of these optimizations enables the full end-to-end pipeline to sustain a measured throughput of 31.3\,FPS on the test hardware, satisfying the real-time processing requirement for 30\,FPS maritime surveillance video.

\section{Inferences}
% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------

Several key inferences are drawn from the experimental results and 
qualitative observations presented in this chapter.

\textbf{Inference 1 — LSTM trajectory prediction substantially outperforms kinematic extrapolation for non-linear vessel motion.}
The directly measured ADE improvement of 28.0\% (44.90\,px vs 62.37\,px) and FDE improvement of 22.2\% (90.28\,px vs 116.02\,px) over the kinematic baseline confirms that the Bi-directional LSTM captures non-linear vessel dynamics that a linear model fundamentally cannot represent. At the pixel-to-metre conversion factor of 0.4\,m/px used in the CPA module, the FDE improvement of 25.74\,px corresponds to approximately 10.3\,m of positional accuracy at step 20 — a gap that directly affects whether a near-miss is correctly classified as critical ($<$50\,m) or high-risk ($<$100\,m). The improvement is most pronounced for curved trajectories (lane changes, turning manoeuvres), which are precisely the cases where accurate prediction matters most for collision alerting.

\textbf{Inference 2 — The AIS-matched LSTM skip optimization is the most impactful single architectural decision for compute efficiency.}
The measured savings of 91.3\% prediction latency at a 60\% AIS match rate and 95.4\% at 80\% demonstrate that selective inference routing — running LSTM only on vessels that genuinely need it — is far more effective than optimising the LSTM model itself. The full-LSTM baseline (all 6 vessels) consumed 86.45\,ms per frame just for prediction; the skip-optimised version reduces this to 7.49\,ms. This 11.5$\times$ speedup requires no model compression, no quantisation, and no approximation: it leverages the semantic meaning of the AIS match status to eliminate redundant computation entirely. No prior maritime surveillance system in the reviewed literature reports an equivalent mechanism.

\textbf{Inference 3 — The CPA collision detector is effectively zero-cost in the pipeline latency budget.}
At 0.446\,ms per frame for 45 vessel pairs (10 vessels), the CPA computation is 33$\times$ faster than the frame read operation (14.90\,ms) and 8$\times$ faster than JPEG encoding (3.12\,ms). This confirms that CPA-based collision detection scales gracefully: even doubling the vessel count to 20 (190 pairs) would add only $\sim$1.9\,ms, preserving real-time headroom. Older systems that avoided real-time collision detection (e.g., Shao et al., Farhadi et al.) did so unnecessarily, as the computational cost is negligible relative to the detection and tracking stages.

\textbf{Inference 4 — The non-YOLO pipeline already exceeds the 30\,FPS real-time target on CPU.}
The measured end-to-end throughput of the pipeline excluding YOLO detection is 42.8\,FPS on CPU-only hardware, demonstrating that every module other than the detector operates well within real-time constraints even without GPU acceleration. This means the system can be deployed on embedded GPU platforms where the YOLO bottleneck is resolved by CUDA acceleration while all other modules run comfortably in parallel, with no pipeline stalling from any non-detection stage.

\textbf{Inference 5 — DeepOCSORT's ReID-augmented tracking provides a decisive identity-preservation advantage.}
The identity switch count of 8 across the test sequences is dramatically lower than SORT (184 IDS, $22.9\times$ more switches), DeepSORT (98 IDS, $12.3\times$), and ByteTrack (61 IDS, $7.6\times$). Identity switches are particularly damaging in maritime surveillance because each new track ID resets the LSTM history buffer, causing the predictor to fall back to kinematic mode and losing all accumulated behavioural context for that vessel. Every identity switch therefore introduces a temporary blind spot in trajectory prediction and collision risk assessment. The 8-switch result confirms that OSNet ReID embeddings provide sufficient discriminative signal to maintain vessel identity across the short-duration occlusions and camera wash-out events common in maritime video.

\textbf{Inference 6 — Multi-modal AIS fusion is a force multiplier for the entire system.}
The integration of AIS data does not merely add a data source — it fundamentally changes the system's operating characteristics across all modules. For detection, AIS provides a ground-truth anchor for matching camera-detected vessels, enabling dark vessel identification. For prediction, it enables the LSTM skip optimization that reduces prediction latency by up to 95.4\%. For collision detection, AIS-reported SOG and COG provide more precise velocity vectors than pixel-space centroid tracking alone, reducing false alert rates. For weather robustness, AIS maintains vessel position continuity during camera visibility loss. No prior work reviewed in Chapter~2 simultaneously exploits all four of these AIS integration benefits.

The weather robustness evaluation further confirms that the proposed system maintains operationally useful performance even under moderate environmental degradation, and that the AIS fusion layer provides meaningful compensation for camera-based detection failures during severe visibility conditions.
